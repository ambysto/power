4. Keep-awake sessions and what Power shows

Power changes one thing on your Mac: whether it may go to sleep when idle, and only while a session or rule that you created is active. Power never limits or changes charging, never changes Energy Saver, Low Power Mode or any sleep setting, never keeps a Mac awake with the lid closed, never writes to the hardware sensors, and never sends signals to or starts other processes.

  • A session ends when you end it, at its deadline, when the battery reaches the battery floor while you are on battery (20 % by default), when the thermal state becomes critical, at its time cap (12 hours by default) unless you answer the question Power asks beforehand, and when Power quits. Keeping a Mac awake uses power and produces heat; you are responsible for starting sessions sensibly.
  • Power's figures are information, not advice. The power-by-app list is an estimate, not a measurement of watts, and does not include the graphics processor. Sensor readings come from per-model tables, and a Mac without one shows no number. Battery health is Power's own ratio of the figures your Mac reports; it is not a service diagnosis or a warranty assessment.
  • Pro alerts only inform you; they never change anything.
  • Use Power only on computers you own or are authorized to manage.
